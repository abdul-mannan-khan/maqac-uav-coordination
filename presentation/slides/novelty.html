What is new

Three ideas, and why each makes MAQAC better

1 · Urgency without a fixed order

Users are valued by how long they have waited, through time-stamped service events, so the objective stays submodular when all UAVs choose at once.

Why it wins: urgent users are reached first. +4–7% over RAG, +7–13% over interference-aware methods that ignore waiting time.

2 · One exchange of service records

UAVs share short records of who was served, instead of negotiating choices. MAQAC sends no message inside a round; MAQAC-S one announcement per UAV.

Why it wins: a lost message costs little. Up to +22% over RAG at 40% message loss, with 5–8× fewer messages.

3 · Guarantee and certificate

A per-round bound for the rule the UAVs run, with perception error and backhaul delay as separate terms, and a Learn-then-Test certificate for each mission.

Why it wins: the operator knows the service level in advance. It held in >97% of test missions and in all 20 flown missions.

Numbers: main grid (9,180 missions), message-loss study on 3 km areas, certificate test and PX4-flown missions; Sections 7.2–7.6 of the paper.

The three ideas work together. Urgency weighting decides what is worth serving, record sharing coordinates without negotiation, and the guarantee and certificate say how good the result is.
