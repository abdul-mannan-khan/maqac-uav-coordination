MAQAC-S vs RAG · pre-registered study, 5,760 missions

When messages are lost or spectrum is shared, MAQAC-S beats RAG

+5–22%

higher score than RAG at 40% message loss, in 8 of 9 configurations on 3 km areas

+4–9%

higher score than RAG when the whole fleet shares one channel

5–8×

fewer in-round transmissions than RAG for the same score on a reliable backhaul

Why: RAG decides through several rounds of gain and commitment messages, so a lost commitment makes two UAVs pick the same users. MAQAC-S sends one announcement per UAV and keeps UAVs apart by its soft partition and co-channel rule.

RAG: Xu, Garimella and Tzoumas, IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2025. Link-limited model, Section 6.8 and Table 5.

The hypothesis was fixed before any run: RAG needs messages inside every decision round, so it should lose ground when messages are lost or channels are scarce. At 40 percent message loss MAQAC-S scored 5 to 22 percent higher than RAG in 8 of the 9 configurations on 3 km areas, most in the disaster scenario. With one shared channel it scored 4 to 9 percent higher in 9 of 18 configurations. If asked: 40 percent loss is a severe condition (measured UAV links lose under 10 percent), and at 20 percent loss the gains appeared in 3 to 4 of 18 configurations.
